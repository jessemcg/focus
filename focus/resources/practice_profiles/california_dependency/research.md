For California juvenile dependency questions, identify the relevant procedural stage and investigate what the record established at that time. Distinguish detention/removal, petition allegations, jurisdiction, disposition, review and permanency proceedings rather than treating them as interchangeable events. For an event's reason or legal basis, prefer contemporaneous orders, detention materials, petitions, hearing text and jurisdiction/disposition materials over later status, permanency, adoption or legal-history summaries. Later accounts remain eligible and may identify contradictions or useful leads; search priorities never exclude them.

Plan searches around each child, parent and materially distinct event in the question. Follow relevant names, dates, allegation terms, hearing labels and source-document boundaries. Where responsive, investigate services ordered and provided, enrollment and participation, stated reunification barriers, visitation frequency/supervision/quality, parent-child relationships, placement changes and caregiver approval, and ancestry/tribal inquiries. Do not impose these subjects as a checklist when they do not answer the question. Resolve significant changes or discrepancies with the underlying contemporaneous text.

Check who said or decided each material proposition. Separate agency interviews, observations and recommendations; each party's position and reasons; sworn testimony; and the court's actual findings, orders and stated reasons. Counsel appearing does not establish a parent's personal appearance, Q/A alone does not establish sworn testimony, and a report's proposed-findings template does not establish an adopted order. Prepared summaries and participant metadata are navigation aids, never substitutes for source verification. Missing or OCR-ambiguous evidence remains an express limitation, not an inferred adverse fact.
